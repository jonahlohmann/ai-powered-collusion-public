Do trading bots learn to collude?

Two self-learning algorithms trade on the same private information in a simulated market. Nobody tells them to cooperate, and they cannot talk. If they still end up trading less aggressively than competition would force them to, they earn more at everybody else's expense. This page lets you play with what training sessions found.

05 · Mechanism

Does cheating pay?

Profits above the competitive level are not yet a cartel. A cartel holds only if breaking ranks does not pay, because the other side strikes back. So after training, with learning switched off, one bot is made to cheat for a single period.

07 · Read me

How to read all this

Three yardsticks for ΔC

  • A perfect cartel earns only % more than competition, so ΔC stretches a small difference in profit over the range from 0 to 1.
  • The bots choose among levels. The Nash equilibrium of that grid lies at ΔC , not exactly at 0 ( on the grid of the earlier specification).
  • Two bots that pick levels purely at random reach ΔC on the baseline grid. A positive ΔC alone is no sign of learning to collude.

What this page does not show

  • No run varies the action grid, the exploration counter and the initial Q-values one at a time.
  • The gap to the level that DGJ report is not explained by the decay of exploration, the value grid or the noise level. The layout of the price grid and the length of learning remain open.
  • The mechanism behind the baseline's ΔC is diagnosed, not proven.
  • One learner stands for each family, in one market regime. Nothing here is a statement about DGJ's code.
